# EXP-0025 — Complete-block time attribution

## Approval and status

Approved by the user on 2026-08-28 after EXP-0024 showed that 42.72% of the
W4F16 complete-block DSP ticks were outside the existing named stage totals.
This is a diagnostic experiment, not an optimization or baseline-promotion
experiment. It uses the completed local-pass EXP-0024 source directly without
changing EXP-0024's pending adoption state.

## Objective

Replace the residual subtraction called “unattributed time” with an auditable
Block Timing Ledger whose mutually exclusive intervals cover one complete real
Qwen3-1.7B layer-14, `M=64` invocation exactly once. Separate additive DSP wall
time from overlapping DMA, HVX, and HMX Engine Work Counters, then identify the
largest true block activities that should guide the next optimization.

The experiment must answer two questions with fresh same-boot evidence:

1. Which concrete block activities account for the previous approximately
   483,000-tick residual?
2. Within Attention, how much exclusive wall time belongs to QK preparation
   and HMX, scale/mask and standard Softmax, probability preparation, AV HMX,
   and the surrounding head/group handoffs?

- F16F16 and W4F16 arithmetic, tensor layout, projection pipeline, common HVX

- The Execution Unit remains the complete real layer-14 block with `M=64`, one

- Both variants request and receive exactly 8,388,608 VTCM bytes. Legal DDR is

  synchronization point inside a hot loop.
- Existing Engine Work Counters remain diagnostic and may overlap. They must
  never be summed into the exclusive Block Timing Ledger.

## Timing-ledger design

Record adjacent timestamps at existing block boundaries so the top-level
ledger telescopes from the block-entry timestamp to the block-exit timestamp.
Every interval belongs to exactly one category. At minimum the ledger must
distinguish invocation prologue, boundary input movement, both residual paths,
the existing nine named compute stages, boundary output movement, and
invocation epilogue. Any remaining interval is explicitly named Unattributed
Gap rather than silently inferred by subtracting overlapping counters.

Add a nested, non-additive Attention ledger whose adjacent intervals distinguish
Q/K/V preparation, QK HMX envelope, scale/mask plus stable natural-exponential
Softmax, probability preparation, AV HMX envelope, output/head assembly, and
attention handoff gaps. Nested Attention entries must sum to the top-level
Attention interval, but must not be added again to the complete-block total.

The implementation must retain a runtime switch or paired build path that
executes identical kernels with detailed attribution disabled. The difference
between enabled and disabled fresh medians measures instrumentation
perturbation; the EXP-0024 result alone is not an acceptable overhead control.

## Gates and evidence

- The exclusive top-level ledger closes to the measured block envelope within
  timestamp arithmetic precision. Named entries must cover at least 99% of the
  envelope; Unattributed Gap must be at most 1% for both variants and both
  repeat-one and repeat-ten medians.
- The nested Attention ledger closes to its parent Attention interval within
  timestamp arithmetic precision.
- Attribution-enabled Host wall latency may not regress by more than 2% from
  the paired attribution-disabled median for either variant at repeat-ten
  scope. If it does, the evidence is perturbative and the experiment must not
  claim a bottleneck ranking.
- F16F16 and W4F16 outputs remain finite with cosine at least 0.99999 and
  maximum absolute error at most 0.0625 against their implementation
  references. Attribution on/off output hashes must match for each variant.
- Exact full-VTCM, zero intermediate DDR, zero spill/fill, stable boot,
  Block Package hash, no-QNN, and immutable-artifact gates remain mandatory.

Run one correctness record and five repeat-one plus five repeat-ten measured
invocations per variant for attribution off and on, all on the same boot with
unmeasured warm-up. Equivalent Host wall per block remains primary. Report the
exclusive timing ledger, the separate overlapping engine counters,
instrumentation overhead, and the resulting bottleneck ranking. This
experiment may recommend a next direction but may not implement it.

F16F16 closes at 1,151,261.4 invocation ticks with a 3.3-tick gap. W4F16
closes at 1,130,791.7 invocation ticks with a 3.1-tick gap, or about 0.000274%
of the envelope. The old approximately 42.7% residual is therefore not an
unseen compiler or engine-scheduling interval. It is almost entirely the two
previously untimed residual-add stages. After subtracting their own numerical
audits, those two FP16 residual additions consume 470,922.8 W4F16 ticks, or
41.645% of the complete block.

The experiment also exposes a second measurement issue: the current formal
block path always performs full-tensor numerical reference scans. Across all
stages these audits consume 419,604.4 W4F16 ticks, or 37.107% of the measured
block. They are research validation work rather than Qwen3 inference
arithmetic. This experiment retains them because removing them would be an
optimization and would violate the fixed diagnostic contract.

Purely subtracting those measured audit intervals leaves an estimated
711,187.3-tick inference envelope. Renormalized against that estimate, the two
residual additions account for about 66.22%, Attention for 21.51%, and the four
projection groups for 6.62%. This is decision guidance rather than a measured
audit-free performance run: removing the scans may change scheduling and must
be tested as a separate experiment.

Attention audits total 296,133.1 ticks, about 65.94% of the Attention stage.
After excluding them, actual Attention arithmetic and movement consume
152,957.9 ticks, or 13.527% of the complete block. QK and AV packing together
consume 145,722.6 ticks, or 12.887% of the block, whereas the conventional
Softmax itself is only 0.470%. Softmax is therefore not the next performance
bottleneck in this standalone runtime; Crouton preparation dominates the real
Attention path.

## Interpretation and next-decision input

The attribution work closes the measurement defect rather than creating a new
performance result. The current ranking is now explicit: always-on numerical
audits are the largest removable research overhead, the two scalar residual
adds are the largest real inference activity, and QK/AV packing is the largest
real Attention activity. Projection remains approximately 10.07% of the W4F16
envelope and is no longer the leading complete-block target.

A subsequent optimization experiment should first separate correctness and
performance invocations so full-tensor audits do not execute in the timed
steady state. It should then evaluate HVX residual addition or a fused
residual-plus-RMSNorm pass, followed by Attention packing or a pipelined
QK-to-Softmax-to-AV schedule. Rewriting Softmax alone is not supported by this
evidence.

measurement. EXP-0024 remains pending user adoption, EXP-0023 remains the
