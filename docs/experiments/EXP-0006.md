# EXP-0006 — Chunked multi-HVX W4 expansion and HMX pipeline

## Approval

Approved by the user on 2026-08-27 with the explicit requirement to exploit
HVX and HMX concurrency so that packed-weight expansion can be covered by
other work.

## Objective

Refactor only the physical schedule of the accepted EXP-0005 W4U8 projection
substrate. Replace its serialized `DMA wait -> full output-tile expansion ->
HMX publication` path with a chunked pipeline in which a dedicated User-DMA
producer, multiple HVX expansion workers, and the ordered integer-HMX consumer
can be active concurrently. The experiment asks whether project-owned
scheduling can reduce the packed-W4 wall latency for both real Qwen3
projection shapes.

## Fixed numerical and storage contract

The arithmetic, shapes, activation zero point, packed W4 bytes, per-output
integer scales, correction bias, Implementation Reference, final output, and
legal DDR boundaries remain byte-identical to EXP-0005. Gate/up remains
`M=64, K=2048, N=6144`; down remains `M=64, K=6144, N=2048`. The complete
activation and final output remain VTCM-resident. Packed W4 bundles and compact
status/timing metadata are legal DDR boundaries; expanded S8 chunks and HMX
accumulators are not.

## Physical-plan variants

`exp0005_full_bundle_control` preserves the completed parent implementation.
The chunked variants split each output tile's K depth into chunks of at most 32
architectural HMX K tiles. Gate/up therefore has two chunks per output tile and
down has six. HMX loads the output-tile correction bias and clears its
accumulator once, consumes chunks in strict increasing-K order, and stores the
final saturated U8 tile only after the final chunk.

A dedicated producer stages compressed bundles through User DMA. Independent
HVX workers expand disjoint K chunks into a bounded VTCM slot pool. The single
HMX consumer may begin after the first required chunk is published and releases
each slot after consumption. Compressed-slot ownership lasts until all of its
chunks have completed expansion. Candidate worker counts are exactly 1, 2, 4,
and 6; unsupported counts are recorded as hardware-resource observations and
do not silently fall back to fewer workers.

The implementation records compact proof counters and timestamps sufficient to
show the maximum simultaneous HVX workers and whether HVX expansion overlapped
HMX execution. Performance collection uses the same worker-count search set,
run matrix, warm-up rule, and Host-wall selection rule for both shapes.

## Milestones and gates

The first milestone is ordered chunk correctness with one HVX worker. It must
match the EXP-0005 control and independent Host reference byte-for-byte before
multi-worker timing is considered. The second milestone evaluates 2, 4, and 6
HVX workers, proves that the selected plan executed its requested count without
fallback, and establishes physical HVX/HMX overlap when the device exposes the
necessary resources.

The hard validity gate requires V79 DSP and Host builds, identifiable User DMA,
HVX W4 unpack/scale, and integer-HMX instructions, no QNN/QAIRT dependency, zero
output mismatches across both shapes and the inherited four data patterns, zero
DSP/resource/DMA/synchronization errors, no hidden DDR intermediate, and a
stable device boot identifier. The local performance gate passes only if the
selected valid chunked plan improves the primary repeat-10 Host wall median
over `exp0005_full_bundle_control` for both gate/up and down. Matching or
beating the Expanded-S8 Control is reported but is not required.

Failure to beat the parent path remains valid negative evidence. This
experiment does not change W4 quantization, add arbitrary real scales, implement
a complete MLP or block, or establish a Selected Baseline.

## Result

Execution completed on source commit
`36545d8ede6f38f6443c220d65b3014c3f628e4f`, and the local correctness,
physical-overlap, and performance gates passed. The formal matrix contains 48
repeat-1 correctness records and 144 repeat-10 timing records. It covers both
real projection shapes, all four inherited patterns, the EXP-0005 packed-W4
control, 1/2/4/6-worker chunked candidates, and the Expanded-S8 observation
control. All 192 runs returned zero mismatches and zero DSP, DMA, resource, or
synchronization errors. Outputs and expanded S8 carrier identities are equal
across every storage and schedule variant. The device boot identifier remained
`283cc037-8da2-4e83-bf6f-05429beb7d90`.

Static evidence identifies User DMA, the HVX nibble LUT, signed byte multiply,
saturated pack, independent chunk-worker entry points, ordered integer-HMX
accumulation, and final saturated U8 storage. No QNN or QAIRT dynamic dependency
is present. The runtime reports six available 128-byte HVX contexts. Every
requested worker count was created and locked without fallback, and all
multi-worker variants observed both HVX-to-HVX and HVX-to-HMX overlap.

The primary Host-wall result selects `chunked_hvx6` for both shapes. Gate/up
falls from a 66.866 ms packed full-bundle median to 42.031 ms, a 37.14%
improvement. Down falls from 52.224 ms to 24.762 ms, a 52.59% improvement. The
DSP pipeline median shows the same effect: gate/up falls from 1,095,775 to
616,074 qtimer ticks, while down falls from 881,294.5 to 347,862. HMX ready-wait
falls from 961,769.5 to 520,596.5 ticks for gate/up and from 806,509.5 to
249,406.5 for down.

Summed HVX worker time increases rather than disappears: gate/up reports about
1.246M worker ticks and down about 1.400M. The speedup therefore comes from
concurrency, not from making the expansion arithmetic cheaper. Gate/up has two
K chunks per output and two compressed slots, so at most four expansion tasks
are simultaneously exposed; its six-worker plan normally observes four active
workers. Down has six K chunks and can expose all six workers. This explains
why down scales further and nearly hides the expansion, while gate/up saturates
between four and six workers.

Against the Expanded-S8 observation, gate/up packed W4 remains 31.40% slower
(42.031 versus 31.987 ms), whereas down is within 2.71% (24.762 versus 24.109
ms). Thus EXP-0006 proves that explicit DMA/HVX/HMX pipelining repairs most of
the parent W4 scheduling loss and can nearly cover expansion for the deeper-K
shape; it does not prove that expansion is universally free or establish a
Selected Baseline.
