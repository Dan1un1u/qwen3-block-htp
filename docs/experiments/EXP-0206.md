# EXP-0206 — Direct-W4 decode QKV batch32 command granularity

## Decision and purpose

EXP-0206 inherits the evidence-valid EXP-0205 candidate at 44.261504 token/s.
Its direct-W4 QKV ring still uses batch16, producing eight HMX groups per layer:
four for Q and two each for K and V. The candidate uses batch32, producing two
Q groups and one K and V group per layer. The same one-MiB HMX-accessible
phase-dead weight slots proven by Gate/Up and LM-head batch32 carry each group.

This is not assumed to win from fewer commands alone. Larger QKV groups delay
per-head Q/K readiness and may reduce overlap with preparation. The experiment
therefore directly tests whether command/DMA granularity or head-readiness
latency dominates the complete token path.

## Controlled implementation and gates

One binary compares inherited QKV batch16 with batch32 only for logical-M1
decode. Batch32 may extend the bounded User-DMA helper to split one MiB of
packed W4 into architecture-proven descriptor sizes, but may not change bytes,
arithmetic, tensor values, projection order, Q/K preparation, Attention, O,
MLP, residual, cache, LM head or M64 prefill.

Correctness requires byte-exact Q/K/V carriers, prepared Q/K, hidden state,
cache, selected codes and tokens. Physical evidence requires exact 8 MiB VTCM,
zero timed intermediate DDR, zero spill/fill, one FastRPC, one HMX owner, no
QNN, zero explicit W4-to-S8 expansion, identical packed-W4 bytes and HMX tile
pairs, QKV HMX groups falling from 224 to 112 per token and fewer total DMA
descriptors. A five-pair short gate must improve both QKV-plus-preparation wall
and directly measured full decode token/s before ten formal pairs run. The
user-defined consecutive local-win/E2E-regression stop gate remains active.
