# EXP-0020 — HVX unpack and two-producer streaming dispatch

## Approval

Approved by the user on 2026-08-27 after accepting EXP-0019. The experiment
continues the successful true HVX-to-HMX streaming architecture; it may not
reintroduce EXP-0018 phase separation or replace the selected two-producer
gate/up plan with a logical cap implemented by additional spinning workers.

## Objective

EXP-0019 proves that paired gate/up is fastest when exactly two HVX producers
publish ordered 32-K-tile generations into one persistent HMX consumer. At
repeat ten, this schedule reduces Host wall by 17.36% versus the EXP-0017
coarse-publication parent, but HMX still waits 62.0K qtimer ticks for producer
readiness. The remaining critical path is therefore the rate and fixed
dispatch cost of the two HVX producers, not HMX arithmetic.

This experiment evaluates two independent optimizations. First, replace the
current two-vlut signed-nibble decode with a mathematically identical raw W4
sign-extension sequence when V79 HVX instruction cost is lower. Second,
specialize the selected two-producer schedule so region assignment and
publication do not pay a contended shared-queue mutex/semaphore round trip for
every 32-tile region. The combined candidate is tested only after each isolated
change is exact and device-stable.

## Fixed contract

The primary scope is the EXP-0019 paired gate/up projection
`M=64, K=2048, N=12288` using packed signed W4, asymmetric U8 activation,
byte-identical weights and per-output integer scales, HMX postscale, exactly
two locked 128-byte HVX producers, 32-tile generation-tagged publication, one
persistent ordered integer-HMX consumer, the same 2 MiB VTCM map, linked
weight/output User DMA, one prepared FastRPC invocation, and the same legal DDR
boundaries. HMX starts after a partial region is ready and remains concurrent
with HVX; no whole-group barrier is permitted.

The down projection remains the accepted EXP-0019 control with its 96-tile
coarse publication and six workers. It receives a correctness and regression
check but is not silently switched to the losing 32-tile schedule. No QNN or
QAIRT dependency, weight representation, activation quantization, scale
placement, output order, resource lifetime, or Host/DSP boundary may change.

## Candidates and gates

The unpack candidate must produce the same S8 carrier byte-for-byte for all 16
nibble values and all inherited device patterns. Static disassembly must prove
that its claimed instruction sequence is emitted and that integer HMX remains
present. The dispatch candidate must preserve FIFO generation order, bounded
producer lead, slot lifetime, timeout behavior, and exactly two physical HVX
workers. It may use per-worker single-producer/single-consumer queues or an
equivalent non-contended mechanism, but may not busy-spin idle extra workers.

Bring-up uses one repeat for every isolated candidate before composition. A
candidate with a mismatch, stale generation, timeout, reboot, fallback, DMA,
resource, or synchronization failure is discarded. Formal timing uses rotated
repeat-one and repeat-ten Host-wall medians over all inherited patterns. A new
gate/up plan is retained only if the same candidate beats the EXP-0019
`stream32_gate_hvx2` parent at both repeat counts. The down parent must remain
byte-exact and may not regress beyond timing noise. DSP stage ticks explain the
result, while equivalent-scope Host wall decides. Only the user may accept the
experiment or promote a Selected Baseline.
