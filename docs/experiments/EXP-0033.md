## Motivation

In the EXP-0032 repeat-ten formal matrix, the selected W4F16 complete block is
5.83 percent faster in Host wall than the fastest fair F16F16 batch-four
control. Down is 29.91 percent faster and O projection is 14.79 percent faster;
Gate/Up is 0.68 percent faster. QKV is the only major projection scope in which
W4F16 is slower: 8,500.6 versus 7,675.0 ticks, a 10.76 percent regression that
occupies 19.00 percent of the W4F16 Block Timing Ledger.

The accepted QKV-readiness schedule statically reserves one HVX worker for Q/K
Norm and RoPE while the main context plus two workers retain W4 expansion. This
partition cannot adapt when one queue is temporarily empty or when the next
HMX command is about to run out of expanded weight. EXP-0033 tests whether a
generation-tagged priority scheduler can keep the W4 HMX producer fed while
allowing otherwise-idle HVX capacity to consume ready Q/K heads.

## Fixed contract

- The Execution Unit remains one complete real Qwen3-1.7B layer-14 block at
  `M=64`, measured at repeat one and repeat ten in one FastRPC invocation.
- The F16F16 batch-four control and W4F16 EXP-0032 control retain identical
  weights, scales, projection arithmetic, output layout, HMX commands,
  head-readiness semantics, Q/K Norm, RoPE, GQA Attention, residuals, streaming
  MLP, Gate/Up DMA-8 scale-cache plan, Down, and output arithmetic.
- The candidate may change only HVX worker ownership and queue priority during
  W4F16 Q, K, and V projection plus Q/K Norm-RoPE consumption. It may not fuse
  Q/K/V weights, reorder projection arithmetic, modify HMX command shapes,
  change head mathematics, or extend readiness into QK/Softmax/AV in this
  experiment.
- Every variant requests and receives exactly 8,388,608 VTCM bytes. Q, K, V,
  all weights and expansion buffers, scores, probability, and block
  intermediates retain the existing zero-intermediate-DDR contract. Spill/fill,
  extra FastRPC calls, QNN, and QAIRT are hard failures.
- One serialized HMX owner is retained. Worker work stealing must use explicit
  generation-tagged release/acquire dependencies and may not allow two workers
  to expand or normalize the same unit of work.
- Candidate output must remain byte-identical to the paired EXP-0032 W4F16
  control and pass the independent Implementation Reference and non-finite
  checks.
- Equivalent-scope complete-block Host wall is primary. QKV exclusive ticks,
  W4 expansion readiness, HMX starvation, Q/K task completion, worker idle,
  and join-tail counters are diagnostic and may not be summed as latency.

## Ordered milestones

### A — Worker timeline audit

Record generation-tagged Q/K/V projection phase, packed-weight DMA completion,
expansion job claim and publication, HMX command-ready and command-done events,
Q/K head publication and claim, per-worker queue-empty intervals, and final
Norm-RoPE join tail. Audit instrumentation is excluded from formal performance
collection. The audit must determine whether the QKV regression is caused by
HMX starvation, static worker reservation, head-consumer tail, or an unrelated
projection cost before schedule changes are retained.

### B — Priority work stealing

Replace the fixed W4F16 `two expansion workers plus one head worker` ownership
with a bounded priority policy. Work required to maintain the declared
expanded-weight lookahead for the next HMX command has priority. Once that
lookahead is satisfied, any eligible HVX worker may claim one published Q/K
Norm-RoPE task. The scheduler must return capacity to expansion before the HMX
producer starves and must preserve existing worker-phase barriers outside QKV.

Only queue ownership and priority may change. A fused QKV carrier, different
weight order, direct QKV-to-GQA execution, or changed HMX tiling requires a new
user-approved experiment rather than silent expansion of EXP-0033.

### C — Complete-block selection gate

Collect audit-on correctness and alternating audit-off repeat-one/repeat-ten
timing for the immutable F16F16 control, immutable W4F16 control, and retained
dynamic candidate in one stable device boot. At both repeat counts, a selected
candidate must reduce W4F16 QKV exclusive ticks and complete-block W4F16 Host
wall relative to its control. It must not regress F16F16, must keep the W4F16
complete block strictly faster than F16F16, and must preserve byte-identical
output, exact 8 MiB VTCM, zero intermediate DDR, zero spill/fill, one FastRPC,
and one HMX owner.

QKV parity with F16F16 is a desired diagnostic target, not a separate hard
percentage gate. If dynamic worker ownership cannot pass the complete-block
gate, stop with valid negative evidence instead of proceeding to fused QKV.
