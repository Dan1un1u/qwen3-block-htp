# EXP-0028 — Early-region W4F16 DMA--HVX--HMX pipeline

## Approval and status

Approved by the user on 2026-08-28 after EXP-0027 established a fair,
double-buffered batch-two F16F16 control. EXP-0028 repairs the W4F16 projection
pipeline before any shared Attention, SiLU, RMSNorm, RoPE, or residual work.
It does not accept EXP-0027, promote a Selected Baseline, or change EXP-0023's
role as the Provisional Source Parent.

## Motivation

EXP-0027 showed that F16F16 projection is weight-flow dominated, but its former
serial control was materially under-optimized. The fair F16F16 schedule takes
43,521.5 projection ticks and 12.3994115 ms per complete block. The unchanged
W4F16 control reads only one quarter as many weight bytes yet takes 46,675.3
projection ticks and 12.5856146 ms because its 35,275.2-tick expansion work and
38,921.8-tick HMX hand-off/wait envelope are not sufficiently hidden.

The experiment tests whether publishing bounded expanded regions as soon as
they are ready, and controlling producer lookahead explicitly, can make HMX
consume useful W4-derived work without waiting for a whole superchunk or
allowing HVX to run too far ahead.

## Ordered milestones

### A — Close the W4 producer/consumer timeline

Instrument, without printing in the hot loop, the ordered timestamps and wait
counts for compressed-weight DMA completion, each HVX region publication, HMX
command publication, HMX completion, slot release, and producer throttling.
Separate exclusive projection latency from overlapping DMA, expansion, and HMX
engine counters. Identify whether the critical bubbles are before first HMX,
between HMX commands, or at slot reuse.

### B — Early-region hand-off

Evaluate at least one Physical Plan in which HMX begins after the minimum
architecturally consumable expanded region is ready rather than after the full
resident superchunk is expanded. Generation-tagged readiness and explicit
publication barriers must prevent stale reads. The producer may not overwrite
a region until its HMX consumer completes.

If the HMX accumulation or Crouton address contract makes partial-region
consumption invalid, record that as a physical limitation and retain the
smallest legal hand-off granularity; do not emulate the missing behavior with
DDR or extra FastRPC calls.

### C — Bounded lookahead search

Search a compact set of producer credits or lookahead distances while keeping
the same W4 bytes, HMX batch-two command count, worker count, and exact 8 MiB
grant unless a changed value is itself the declared single variable. The goal
is to overlap DMA, HVX, and HMX without letting HVX monopolize VTCM bandwidth
or finish an entire group before HMX starts.

### D — Fair selection and stop point

Select the fastest valid W4F16 candidate that improves both repeat-one and
repeat-ten Host wall over the EXP-0027 W4 control. The local speed gate then
requires the selected W4 candidate to be strictly faster than EXP-0027's
F16F16 batch-two control for both projection and complete-block Host wall at
repeat one and repeat ten. A stretch target is at least 20% projection and 5%
complete-block improvement over that F16 control.

If no W4 candidate passes the strict local gate, stop and report the remaining
critical path before any shared-operator optimization. If it passes, a later
experiment may return to QK/AV packing with this W4 plan frozen.

## Implementation and schedule search

Source commit `6ba4e75de485a3070397342b8e4ffe09c8dbe15e` adds
ordered producer/consumer counters, an early-region HMX hand-off probe, compact
worker/region searches, cross-projection first-batch prefetch, and the formal
evidence matrix. The mathematical W4 values, per-channel scales, FP16 HMX
path, common operators, residual path, and legal DDR boundary are unchanged.

The literal early-region hypothesis failed. Publishing each 32-K-tile region
and starting the streaming HMX consumer before a complete two-output-tile
group was expanded increased the preliminary projection path to about 53.25K
ticks and the complete block to about 12.92 ms. HMX readiness and tail waits
grew instead of disappearing. This identifies the minimum useful physical
handoff as the complete two-output-tile group for the current HMX accumulation
path; region publication alone cannot recover the compiler-like fine-grained
schedule.

The successful repair came from load balance rather than finer handoff. For
Q/K/V/O and Gate/Up, two HVX workers plus the FastRPC thread with 32-tile
regions minimized expansion latency. Down has three times the input width, so
three workers plus the FastRPC thread and four equal 96-tile regions are
faster. Six legal first batches are also prefetched into the otherwise idle
compressed-weight VTCM slot across adjacent projection boundaries. Their
measured exposed wait is only a few qtimer ticks; no expanded tensor or block
intermediate crosses DDR.

## Interpretation and status

EXP-0028 restores the physical argument for compressed weights: after fair
tuning, W4F16 reads one quarter of the FP16 weight bytes and is again faster
than the optimized F16F16 path. The result also bounds the remaining upside.
At repeat ten, shared Attention still takes about 152.9K ticks and SiLU takes
about 32.3K ticks per block, while the entire selected W4 projection path is
37.35K ticks. Consequently a substantial projection improvement is diluted to
2.59% at the complete-block boundary.

Execution is complete, evidence is valid, and the local gate passes. Adoption
remains pending: the result is not a Selected Baseline and EXP-0023 remains the
Provisional Source Parent until the user explicitly changes either decision.
