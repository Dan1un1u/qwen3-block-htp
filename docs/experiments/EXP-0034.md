# EXP-0034 — Q/K/V projection critical-path attribution

## Approval and status

Approved by the user on 2026-08-28 after EXP-0033 established that dynamic
QKV worker ownership is counterproductive. The experiment starts from the
clean EXP-0032 source commit
`91ba193660513bd0675c0a091db199aa09df1773`, not from the rejected EXP-0033
implementation. Using EXP-0032 as an experimental source parent does not
change its pending adoption decision or promote a Selected Baseline.

## Motivation

EXP-0033 showed that the accepted Q/K Norm-RoPE readiness consumer already
uses a fourth HVX context and does not take one of the three W4 expansion
contexts away from QKV. Main-context work stealing removes only a small final
join tail and instead delays later projection/HMX producer work. The aggregate
QKV interval nevertheless remains about eleven percent slower for W4F16 than
for F16F16, while O, Down, and Gate/Up are at parity or faster.

The aggregate interval cannot determine whether the remaining regression is
caused by the large `2048 -> 2048` Q projection, the two half-width
`2048 -> 1024` K/V projections, or a cost shared by all three. EXP-0034 adds an
equivalent-scope per-projection ledger before choosing another schedule.

## Fixed contract

- The Execution Unit remains one complete real Qwen3-1.7B layer-14 block at
  `M=64`, measured at repeat one and repeat ten in one FastRPC invocation.
- Controls are the EXP-0032 F16F16 batch-four plan and selected W4F16
  Gate/Up batch-four, DMA-8, scale-cache plan. Q/K head readiness, GQA
  Attention, streaming MLP, HMX command shapes, weights, scales, arithmetic,
  output layout, and all operator implementations remain unchanged.
- The source ABI may add telemetry only. It may not change scheduling, worker
  ownership, projection order, packed-weight layout, HMX tiling, or numerical
  work in this experiment.
- Every configuration requests and receives exactly 8,388,608 VTCM bytes.
  All intermediates remain in VTCM or accelerator state. Intermediate DDR,
  spill/fill, extra FastRPC calls, QNN, and QAIRT are hard failures.
- F16F16 and W4F16 outputs must remain byte-identical to their EXP-0032
  controls and pass the independent Implementation Reference.
- Per-projection wall intervals are exclusive sequential Q, K, and V call
  boundaries. Engine-work counters such as DMA, expansion, and HMX work may
  overlap and may not be added together as latency.

## Required ledger

For Q, K, and V independently, record projection wall ticks, packed-weight DMA
work, projection pack work, W4 expansion elapsed and worker-work ticks,
expansion pool wait, HMX compute work, HMX readiness wait, prefetch wait,
unpack work, and HMX command count. Record the unattributed difference between
the aggregate QKV interval and the sum of the three projection wall intervals.

The ledger must be collected symmetrically for F16F16 and W4F16. Counters that
do not apply to F16F16 must be exactly zero rather than omitted. Formal
performance collection uses audit-off runs, but telemetry remains enabled for
both controls so its overhead is paired.

## Completion gate

Collect one audit-on correctness run and five alternating audit-off timing
runs at repeat one and repeat ten for each control in one stable boot. The
experiment passes its attribution gate only if:

- every record preserves byte-identical output and the fixed physical
  contract;
- Q, K, and V projection wall intervals are positive and their sum plus the
  explicitly reported unattributed interval equals aggregate QKV ticks;
- engine counters reconcile with the existing aggregate counters; and
- the paired medians identify whether the W4F16 regression is dominated by Q,
  K/V, or a shared per-projection mechanism.

This is an attribution gate, not a speed-selection gate. No QKV packing,
fusion, tiling, or scheduling candidate may be retained in EXP-0034. After the
ledger is complete, choose exactly one follow-up hypothesis with the user. If
the ledger cannot distinguish the source, stop and report it as inconclusive.

## Implementation

Source commit `c39dfefdb24c1bb9670a263a6605f833653800cc`
adds telemetry only. It snapshots the existing aggregate projection counters
immediately before each Q, K, and V call and records their deltas after that
call completes. The snapshot includes HMX worker compute and ready-wait state,
so it does not require instrumentation inside HMX, HVX, or User DMA kernels.

Each call-boundary stage includes work physically initiated or completed in
that stage, including cross-prefetch for the following projection. This is
intentional: the ledger represents the real critical-path schedule rather than
assigning asynchronous work to a logical tensor after the fact. A separate
unattributed field records Q/K Norm-RoPE join and other work outside the three
projection call boundaries. For every record, Q wall plus K wall plus V wall
plus unattributed ticks equals aggregate QKV ticks exactly.

## Formal outcome

The formal matrix contains 22 records in one stable device boot: one audit-on
correctness record plus five alternating audit-off repeat-one and repeat-ten
records for each control. Medians below are per complete block; lower is
better.

| Repeat | Variant | Q | K | V | Outside calls | Aggregate QKV |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | F16F16 | 3,878.0 | 1,912.0 | 1,888.0 | 27.0 | 7,679.0 |
| 1 | W4F16 | 4,813.0 | 1,617.0 | 1,640.0 | 504.0 | 8,541.0 |
| 10 | F16F16 | 3,871.6 | 1,913.4 | 1,888.8 | 24.9 | 7,698.5 |
| 10 | W4F16 | 4,901.7 | 1,636.4 | 1,642.7 | 279.4 | 8,462.5 |

At repeat ten, W4F16 Q is 1,030.1 ticks or 26.61 percent slower than F16F16.
K is 277.0 ticks or 14.48 percent faster and V is 246.1 ticks or 13.03
percent faster. The outside-call tail adds another 254.5 ticks. These values
close to the observed aggregate W4F16 QKV regression of 764.0 ticks. The same
sign pattern repeats at repeat one: Q is the only slow projection.

The Q engine ledger provides a stronger causal clue:

| Repeat 10 Q metric | F16F16 | W4F16 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Projection wall | 3,871.6 | 4,901.7 |
| Weight DDR bytes | 8,388,608 | 2,228,224 |
| Weight DMA lifetime/work | 3,530.3 | 4,545.0 |
| W4 expand elapsed | 0 | 2,594.3 |
| W4 expand worker work | 0 | 1,890.8 |
| HMX wait | 1,812.5 | 2,986.1 |
| HMX compute work | 563.4 | 305.6 |
| Unpack | 936.4 | 141.0 |

W4 Q reads about 73 percent fewer weight bytes and reduces measured HMX
compute and unpack work, yet its DMA lifetime and call wall are larger. W4 Q
expand and HMX wait are approximately twice the K values, as expected from
the two-times output width. Q wall is not: 4,901.7 ticks is about 1,629 ticks
above twice the W4 K wall, while F16 Q is almost exactly twice F16 K. W4 Q DMA
lifetime is similarly about 1,783 ticks above twice W4 K.

The most consistent explanation is a first-projection cold-start boundary.
Q has no preceding projection from which to receive cross-prefetched packed
weights; K and V do. The extra Q wall follows the non-overlapped first packed
weight transfer/lifetime rather than W4 expansion scaling, K/V fragmentation,
or additional HMX arithmetic. This is an inference from the paired stage
ledger, not a claim about undocumented hardware internals.

## Decision state and follow-up boundary

EXP-0034 is `completed`, its evidence is `valid`, its attribution
`local_gate` is `pass`, and adoption is `not_applicable` because no schedule or
runtime candidate was introduced. The evidence rejects K/V packing as the
next primary hypothesis.

The narrowly supported follow-up is to start the first packed Q-weight DMA
before the Q projection and overlap it with the existing input RMSNorm, then
consume that exact prefetched bundle in the unchanged Q projection. That
changes a cross-stage lifetime and therefore requires a new experiment. Its
gate must reduce Q wall and complete-block Host wall at repeat one and repeat
ten while preserving the F16 control, exact arithmetic, HMX commands, fixed
8 MiB VTCM, and zero intermediate DDR.
